Writing samples · one thread: obesity and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)

Samples

1. Manuscript opening (narrative review)

Independent sampleAudience: clinicians in sleep, metabolic and cardiovascular medicine

Background. OSA is characterised by disordered breathing during sleep and is associated with major cardiovascular complications; excess adiposity is an aetiological risk factor.1

Evidence base. The SURMOUNT-OSA programme comprised two phase 3, double-blind, randomised, controlled trials of tirzepatide in adults with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity, one in people unable or unwilling to use positive airway pressure (PAP) and one in people already using it. The primary endpoint was change in the apnoea–hypopnoea index (AHI) from baseline.1,2

Findings. Tirzepatide reduced AHI, body weight, hypoxic burden, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and systolic blood pressure, and improved sleep-related patient-reported outcomes, compared with placebo.1

Limitations. The trials were sponsored by the manufacturer,1 excluded people with diabetes, and lasted 52 weeks; clinically relevant cut-offs for the sleep-symptom measures are not well defined.2 Long-term clinical outcomes were not the primary question.

Conclusion. In adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe OSA, weight-directed pharmacotherapy lowered disease severity over one year; whether this translates into fewer cardiovascular events remains to be shown.

Statements were checked against the NEJM abstract and an ACC trial summary, not the full text. No effect sizes are quoted until checked against the full paper.

2. Plain-language summary

Audience: patients

Sleep apnoea means your breathing repeatedly pauses or becomes shallow while you sleep. Carrying extra weight is one of the known causes. In a one-year trial, adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe sleep apnoea who took a weekly injection of a medicine called tirzepatide had fewer breathing interruptions per hour, and lost weight, compared with those given a dummy treatment. This was true both for people using a CPAP machine and for people who were not. Whether this medicine is right for you is a decision to make with your doctor or sleep clinic, alongside options such as CPAP and weight management.

Content aligned to references 1 and 2. Written at an accessible reading level, with a clear next step for the reader.
